# 2026 Planting Calendar: Free Vegetable Garden Planner by USDA Zone

Planting too early means frost kills your seedlings. Planting too late means your tomatoes never ripen. The difference between a thriving garden and a frustrating one often comes down to timing.

Here is how to find your planting dates and plan your 2026 vegetable garden.

## Step 1: Find your USDA hardiness zone

Enter your ZIP code at **[plantingcalendar.net](https://plantingcalendar.net/)** and it instantly shows your USDA zone and frost dates.

## Step 2: Know your frost dates

- **Last spring frost date**: When you can safely plant warm-season crops outdoors
- **First fall frost date**: Your deadline for harvest before cold kills your plants
- **Frost-free days**: The total growing window between these two dates

Zone 8a (10-15°F winter lows), for example, typically has a last frost around March 15-30 and a first frost around November 15-30, giving about 240 frost-free days. Zone 5b is completely different — last frost in mid-May, first frost in early October, only about 140 days.

## Step 3: Plan by vegetable type

| Vegetable | When to Plant | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes | 2 weeks after last frost | Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost |
| Peppers | 2 weeks after last frost | Need warm soil (65°F+) |
| Carrots | 2-4 weeks before last frost | Direct sow, thin to 2" apart |
| Lettuce | 4 weeks before last frost | Can also plant in late summer for fall harvest |
| Garlic | October-November | Plant cloves in fall, harvest next summer |

## Step 4: Succession planting

Do not plant everything at once. Sow lettuce every 2 weeks for continuous harvest. Plant a second round of beans after the first finishes. This keeps your garden producing all season instead of overwhelming you with everything at once.
