# TDEE Calculator for Bodybuilding: Calories, Macros, and the Numbers That Actually Matter

Most bodybuilders know the formula: calories in vs. calories out. But the missing piece is knowing your actual TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure — not just an estimate from a random website.

## TDEE, BMR, and why the difference matters

- **BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)**: Calories your body burns at complete rest — just keeping you alive
- **TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)**: BMR + calories burned through daily activity, exercise, and digestion
- **The gap between them** is where fat loss or muscle gain happens

If you eat at TDEE, your weight stays the same. Eat 300-500 calories below for a cut. Eat 300-500 above for a bulk. The math is simple. Knowing your actual numbers is the hard part.

## Macro split for bodybuilding phases

| Phase | Protein | Carbs | Fat | Calories vs TDEE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bulking | 1.6-2.2g/kg | 45-60% | 20-30% | +300-500 |
| Cutting | 2.2-2.8g/kg | 30-40% | 20-30% | -300-500 |
| Maintenance | 1.6-2.0g/kg | 40-50% | 25-35% | ±0-100 |

Protein is higher during a cut to preserve muscle mass. Carbs fuel performance during a bulk. These are starting points — track for 2 weeks and adjust.

## Common mistakes

1. **Overestimating activity level** — Most people are "lightly active," not "moderately active." Be honest.
2. **Eating back exercise calories** — Your TDEE already includes activity. Do not add gym calories on top.
3. **Changing too many variables at once** — Change one thing (calories, cardio, macros) and watch for 2 weeks before adjusting again.
