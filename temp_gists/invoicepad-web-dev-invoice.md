# Web Development Invoice Template: What Freelance Developers Should Include in 2026

If you are a freelance web developer sending invoices, you already know the basics: client name, your name, amount due. But a good invoice does more than ask for money — it prevents disputes, speeds up payment, and makes you look professional.

Here is what belongs on every web development invoice in 2026.

## Essential fields

1. **Project breakdown by milestone** — Do not just write "Website — $3,000." Break it down: "Homepage design — $800," "API integration — $1,200," "Responsive QA — $400," "Deployment — $600." This eliminates "what exactly am I paying for?" conversations.

2. **Payment terms with late fee clause** — "Net 15" is fine, but add: "A 1.5% monthly late fee applies to balances past due 30 days." This is standard and enforceable.

3. **Revision scope** — "Includes 2 rounds of revisions. Additional revisions billed at $85/hour." Without this, "just one more change" never ends.

4. **Hosting and maintenance note** — If you are not providing ongoing hosting, say so: "Hosting, domain registration, and post-launch maintenance are client's responsibility unless a separate maintenance agreement is signed."

5. **Technology stack reference** — Mention the stack used (React/Next.js, Node.js, PostgreSQL, etc.) so the next developer who inherits the project knows what they are looking at.
