# Why Frost Dates Matter More Than a Generic Planting Month

A generic instruction such as “plant tomatoes in April” is incomplete. April conditions differ dramatically between Miami, Denver, Boston, and Anchorage.

A usable planting schedule should consider:

- the local last spring frost;
- the first autumn frost;
- whether seeds start indoors or outdoors;
- transplant age;
- crop tolerance to cold;
- the gardener’s USDA zone and local microclimate.

[Planting Calendar](https://plantingcalendar.net/) organizes planting guidance around location and frost timing rather than a single national month.

Treat frost dates as probability estimates, not guarantees. Check the short-term weather forecast before transplanting tender crops.
