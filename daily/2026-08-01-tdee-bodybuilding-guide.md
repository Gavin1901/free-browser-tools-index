# TDEE Calculator for Bodybuilding: A Practical Starting Point

A TDEE estimate is useful for bodybuilding only when it becomes the beginning of a measurement loop, not a number treated as permanent truth.

## Start with a consistent estimate

Use a [free TDEE calculator](https://freetdee.com/) to estimate maintenance calories from age, sex, height, weight, and activity. Choose an activity level that reflects your normal week rather than your hardest training day.

## Match calories to the phase

- Maintenance: begin near estimated TDEE.
- Muscle gain: use a modest surplus rather than an aggressive bulk.
- Fat loss: use a sustainable deficit that still supports training and recovery.

The calculator cannot know your exact non-exercise movement, tracking accuracy, or metabolic adaptation. That is why the starting number must be tested against real data.

## Run a two-week feedback loop

1. Keep calorie tracking reasonably consistent.
2. Weigh under similar morning conditions.
3. Compare weekly averages instead of single weigh-ins.
4. Track gym performance, hunger, sleep, and recovery.
5. Adjust by a small amount only after a stable trend appears.

## Avoid common errors

- Do not add exercise calories twice.
- Do not change calories after one unusual weigh-in.
- Do not confuse rapid scale gain with muscle gain.
- Do not use an activity multiplier based on aspiration.

## Takeaway

A bodybuilding TDEE calculator gives you a baseline. Your two-week weight trend and training performance tell you whether that baseline needs a small adjustment.
